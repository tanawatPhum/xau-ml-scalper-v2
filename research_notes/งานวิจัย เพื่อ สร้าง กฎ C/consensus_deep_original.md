## Mechanisms, Contradictions, and Gaps

### Sessions and Auction Regimes

New York futures contribute more price discovery than London spot on average, despite much smaller trading volume; leadership varies intraday and across years [[13.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/who-sets-the-price-of-gold-london-or-new-york-hauptfleisch-putniņš/17734e61e238597ebb07208aef36e6c9/) [[13.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/who-sets-the-price-of-gold-london-or-new-york-hauptfleisch-putniņš/17734e61e238597ebb07208aef36e6c9/) [[13.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/who-sets-the-price-of-gold-london-or-new-york-hauptfleisch-putniņš/17734e61e238597ebb07208aef36e6c9/). Other evidence identifies futures leadership internationally, rapid transmission across exchanges, and substantial US leadership during news-intensive periods [[14.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/who-leads-in-intraday-gold-price-discovery-and-volatility-sehgal-sobti/d2c71f52b48b55f782d503852bf5e4ff/) [[15.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/information-transmission-among-world-major-gold-futures-fuangkasem-chunhachinda/1501737d025f5211abb8c6464c4ee2cd/) [[16.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/how-do-macroeconomic-news-surprises-affect-roundtheclock-sobti-sehgal/cb93b65257805008af6dde98b1b41b5f/).

Nevertheless, leadership is not universal: Indian spot markets dominate during some shocks, and another supplied study reports spot dominance [[17.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/futures-leads-the-spot-but-why-not-so-when-market-in-shocks-a-saini-sharma/57a568f5029155e9b958fbb548c358a1/) [[18.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/dynamics-of-intraday-price-discovery-and-volatility-jaiswal/c2d1ea6030505a98b59dc48be74661fb/). These findings do not provide a usable lead–lag strategy without synchronized external prices.

Auction evidence also conflicts. Reform research reports reduced futures return predictability, whereas a working paper finds no significant before/after price-volume change [[19.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/transparency-in-commodities-markets-aspris-foley/bb6ff400136352e3b9abba41ec4558f0/) [[20.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/did-the-new-fix-fix-the-fix-nilsson/87ceae46720d5fc385d845425ccf60f6/). Alleged historical PM-fix downward pressure and inferred manipulation therefore cannot be treated as a stable modern public-clock signal [[21.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/fixing-the-fix-for-silver-and-gold-crain-hoelscher/af96dad3b7f15fa0be46c917843c00ed/) [[20.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/did-the-new-fix-fix-the-fix-nilsson/87ceae46720d5fc385d845425ccf60f6/).

Chinese night-session introduction changed both predictive clocks and market quality, reduced overnight jumps, and increased international information flow [[5.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/the-“night-effect”-of-intraday-trading-evidence-from-ma-bouri/65bc2bd054af5a33b67ace935d7084f1/) [[22.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/can-night-trading-sessions-improve-forecasting-yao-hui/9ad85ef2c87e55138428e1e6d6ace5b5/) [[23.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/night-trading-and-market-quality-evidence-from-chinese-and-jiang-kellard/e4bbabce17ef5405908d4273975ed170/). Calendar anomalies likewise differ geographically: Shanghai Monday effects contrast with limited London return seasonality and another study’s Friday/Tuesday pattern [[24.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/identification-of-the-daily-seasonality-in-gold-returns-wang-liu/0dea83a85b3b5d5fb5df4401d579be17/) [[24.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/identification-of-the-daily-seasonality-in-gold-returns-wang-liu/0dea83a85b3b5d5fb5df4401d579be17/) [[25.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/weekday-effects-on-gold-tokyo-london-and-new-york-markets-yu-lee/ccd94388d07a52d0bb844865b73a0702/).

### Large Returns and News

**Large movement does not determine subsequent direction.** Gold-ETF predictability strengthens in high-volatility subsamples, while abnormal-day evidence distinguishes same-day continuation from next-day reversal [[4.7]](https://consensus.app/papers/intraday-return-predictability-evidence-from-commodity-xu-bouri/2fbfff98f36d59e3bf6ec4b2457c10d0/) [[12.5]](https://consensus.app/papers/gold-and-oil-prices-abnormal-returns-momentum-and-caporale-plastun/0b8fe41cbdf75091b86c5cd4ac735780/). Chinese precious-metal futures exhibit both momentum and reversal [[5.3]](https://consensus.app/papers/the-“night-effect”-of-intraday-trading-evidence-from-ma-bouri/65bc2bd054af5a33b67ace935d7084f1/).

Microstructure is consequential: roughly one-third of return variance in one SHFE study is attributed to noise [[26.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/components-of-the-bid–ask-spread-and-variance-a-unified-hagströmer-henricsson/3feaeb75147654c08b7e27293fc9fa67/). Equity evidence—supporting analogy only—attributes short reversals to bid/ask bounce and temporary liquidity imbalance [[27.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/intraday-patterns-in-the-crosssection-of-stock-returns-heston-korajczyk/bf4a85771e455e0a9fc24058f3072002/). Transaction-level models similarly separate public information from quote/trade microstructure effects [[28.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/why-do-security-prices-change-a-transactionlevel-analysis-madhavan-richardson/7f7f0628075f5ff38138b6bb16d698ff/).

Macroeconomic effects are well documented but heterogeneous. Employment, GDP, CPI and personal income materially affect gold volatility; 08:30 announcements often dominate metal-futures responses [[29.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/what-moves-the-gold-market-cai-cheung/12954fb3582359448cbfabaa6c4c3ef5/) [[30.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/impact-of-macroeconomic-news-on-metal-futures-elder-miao/99e0a03789dd5d8c9aceab4cb711605e/). Most reaction completes within 90 seconds in one study, whereas FOMC-related adjustment persists beyond five minutes in another [[31.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/the-importance-of-belief-dispersion-in-the-response-of-gold-smales-yang/9a4ce4cbcd91598eb3483759322cdf84/) [[32.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/how-do-the-gold-intraday-returns-and-volatility-react-to-awartani-hussain/520a384cab0e55b7bce955e0148815f1/). News effects depend on surprise sign, belief dispersion, economic state, and media transmission [[31.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/the-importance-of-belief-dispersion-in-the-response-of-gold-smales-yang/9a4ce4cbcd91598eb3483759322cdf84/) [[16.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/how-do-macroeconomic-news-surprises-affect-roundtheclock-sobti-sehgal/cb93b65257805008af6dde98b1b41b5f/) [[33.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/media-effects-matter-macroeconomic-announcements-in-the-liang-sun/1793516df44b525db96600a24bfe9baf/).

Without verified announcements and surprises, **08:30 is a clock label, not an identified news shock**. Preannouncement-drift evidence concerns Treasury/equity futures, not an established gold signal [[34.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/price-drift-before-us-macroeconomic-news-private-kurov-sancetta/d3fa79bf5509566fb34b2d332f2e03fe/) [[34.2]](https://consensus.app/papers/price-drift-before-us-macroeconomic-news-private-kurov-sancetta/d3fa79bf5509566fb34b2d332f2e03fe/).

## Reproducible Evaluation Protocol

### Data and Execution

Freeze cache hashes, timestamp conventions, duplicate/missing-minute treatment, strategy code, and an experiment ledger. Do not synthesize OHLC, volume, news surprises, intraminute stops, or limit fills.

For quantity \(q\), entry \(e\), exit \(x\):

\[
P_L=q(bid_x-ask_e)-K,\qquad
P_S=q(bid_e-ask_x)-K.
\]

Here \(K\) includes commissions and additional adverse execution costs. **Spread is already included; do not deduct it twice.** Minute closes are indicative quotes, not confirmed fills: FX aggregation research distinguishes displayed spread from effective costs and rejection risk [[41.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/execution-in-an-aggregator-oomen/d754aee7916f58969b4179d7450a55d4/). Quote volatility interacting with latency generates execution-price risk, and OTC costs can vary with client characteristics [[42.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/highfrequency-quoting-shortterm-volatility-in-bids-and-hasbrouck/e5659d90d1535a43bb8bb143507f5460/) [[43.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/discriminatory-pricing-of-overthecounter-derivatives-hau-hoffmann/796d344353b85a8598f6b414ecd80943/).

Prespecify baseline next-minute fills, two-minute delay, additional adverse slippage of 0.25 and 0.50 contemporaneous spreads per side, and a doubled-spread stress. These are proposed sensitivity scenarios, **not empirically calibrated broker costs**. Without fill logs, report “quote-based net simulation,” not realized executable profit.

Schedule all exits early enough to flatten by 16:55 NY. If required quotes are missing, mark execution indeterminate and apply a preregistered conservative handling rule—not a fictitious fill.

### Chronology and Search Controls

Use only 2010–2016 for development, with chronological expanding-window folds and all thresholds fitted on preceding data. Remove training outcomes extending into test periods; historical lookbacks alone are not future leakage. Purging/embargo research supports dependency-aware evaluation, but its supplied empirical applications are outside gold CFDs [[44.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/novel-insights-into-the-modeling-financial-timeseries-khosravi-ghazani/9cf23248bbba51279421617df132de0e/) [[45.1]](https://consensus.app/papers/dynamic-investment-strategies-with-machine-learning-berger/884efb51f27658b59f8a5b9096540ce5/).

### Complementarity and Acceptance

Calculate occupancy **after generating C independently**:

\[
O_C=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N_C}
\mathbf1\{\text{any A or B position open at }e_i\}}{N_C}.
\]

Use the union of A/B open intervals, every direction, with explicit same-timestamp ordering. Require \(O_C\le0.30\); do not suppress overlapping entries. Report numerator, denominator, uncertainty interval, daily net-P&L correlation, concurrent exposure, and combined drawdown.

A candidate needs positive standalone quote-based net P&L, credible uncertainty bounds, delay/cost resilience, and acceptable occupancy. Failure on complementarity is not repaired by profitable gross returns.
